# Building a SellerAmp SAS killer: the complete blueprint

**The single biggest opportunity in Amazon seller tooling is building the first AI-native, multi-marketplace sourcing analysis platform.** SellerAmp SAS dominates the $50–100M arbitrage sourcing tool market at $20/month, but suffers from a broken mobile app, inaccurate sales data, and zero eBay support. No existing tool combines predictive deal intelligence, Amazon + eBay side-by-side analysis, modern mobile scanning, and VA workflow management. A tool built from scratch with these capabilities — starting as a personal tool, then scaling to SaaS — has a genuine path to category leadership by 2027.

The arbitrage/wholesale segment represents **~950K–1.1M active Amazon sellers**, with roughly 200K–300K currently paying for sourcing tools. Meanwhile, 61% of Amazon sellers also sell on at least one other marketplace, with eBay being the #1 alternative. Yet every major sourcing tool is Amazon-only. This cross-marketplace gap, combined with the AI revolution and SellerAmp's documented weaknesses, creates a rare window for a well-executed new entrant.

## What sellers actually hate about SellerAmp SAS

SellerAmp SAS holds a **3.7/5 rating on the App Store** (vs. 4.3/5 for its Chrome extension), and the mobile app is the single largest source of user fury. Real user complaints paint a clear picture of where to win.

**The mobile app is chronically unreliable.** One user wrote: *"Just another day of trying to source products when my SellerAmp app won't load. It's a known issue but it's been going on since I've been using the mobile app, which is well over a year."* Another: *"So annoyed by the fact that it DONT SCAN ANYTHING! Why am I paying you $20 a month?"* After a scanning update, users reported the camera became slower rather than faster — a regression that still generates complaints. For retail arbitrage sellers who live on their phones in store aisles, this is a dealbreaker.

**Data accuracy is the second critical failure.** Chrome-Stats' aggregated review summary notes *"unreliable data accuracy, especially in sales estimations for North America."* One Chrome Web Store reviewer reported the monthly sales estimation feature *"does not work in the USA and Canada"* and received no response despite contacting support through multiple channels. Sellers have flagged phantom sales that don't match Seller Central, BSR showing "Unknown" for clearly active products, and fee calculations that don't match actual Amazon invoices. An independent review from EntreResource noted the IP claims database is *"not as accurate or complete"* as dedicated alternatives like IP Alert.

**Customer support is inconsistent.** Multiple users report critical bugs going unacknowledged for weeks or months. The sales estimation issue in North America was reported *"multiple times"* through support, Instagram, and screenshots — with zero resolution. SellerAmp has only 3 reviews on Trustpilot, suggesting minimal engagement with customer feedback platforms.

Beyond these pain points, SellerAmp lacks several features competitors offer: **no automated deal scoring** (BuyBotPro does this), **no bulk wholesale list analysis** (Tactical Arbitrage and Seller Assistant offer this), **no VA/team management** with sub-accounts and permissions, **no purchase order generation**, only 1 ROI calculation method (vs. BuyBotPro's 6), scan limits on lower plans (1,000/month), and English-only support. These gaps compound into a tool that works acceptably for solo beginners but breaks down for scaling operations.

## The competitive landscape has clear gaps nobody fills

The sourcing tool market splits into three distinct categories: **analysis tools** (SellerAmp, BuyBotPro, AZInsight, RevSeller), **automated sourcing tools** (Tactical Arbitrage, SourceMogul), and **data layers** (Keepa). No single tool spans all three.

**SellerAmp SAS** ($19.95–$27.95/month) is the value leader for on-page deal analysis across Chrome, web, and mobile. It offers profit calculators, Keepa chart integration, restriction alerts, IP risk flags, and storefront search across 7 Amazon marketplaces. Its strength is breadth at an affordable price; its weakness is depth and reliability.

**BuyBotPro** ($17.95–$129.95/month) is the closest direct competitor and the premium option. Its killer feature is **one-click automated deal analysis** that reads and interprets Keepa charts to deliver a buy/pass recommendation with a numerical score. It offers 6 ROI methods, a VA Toolbox, and a unique Suspension Safeguard service. However, users report *"the calculator loads horrendously slow"* and *"the performance has dropped terribly"* after UI updates. It's 2x SellerAmp's price for comparable tiers.

**AZInsight** ($7.95–$19.95/month) is the stealth value play — matching SellerAmp's features at half the price. It includes a Google Sheets add-on, sub-accounts for VAs, Walmart calculator support, and a 30-day free trial with no credit card. Its weakness is brand recognition and a mobile app still in beta.

**Keepa** (~$21/month) is the essential data layer that virtually every other tool depends on. It tracks **5+ billion Amazon products** with price and BSR history updated every 15 minutes. Most sellers pay for both Keepa AND a sourcing tool ($40–48/month combined), creating an opportunity for a tool that reduces this cost burden.

**Tactical Arbitrage** (now $69/month in the Seller 365 bundle with 9 other tools) automates product discovery across **1,400+ online retailers** — a fundamentally different function from analysis. Serious OA sellers use Tactical Arbitrage to FIND leads, then SellerAmp to ANALYZE them. The Seller 365 bundle (TA + InventoryLab + repricing + more for $69) is the most aggressive consolidation play in the market.

**RevSeller** ($99.99/year) is the budget option with a 4.8/5 Chrome rating — dead simple and cheap, but Chrome-only, US/Canada-only, and missing IP alerts, sales estimation, and mobile.

The critical market gaps across ALL tools are:

- **No tool combines automated sourcing + deep analysis** in a single workflow
- **No tool supports Amazon + eBay** for sourcing analysis (every tool is Amazon-only)
- **No tool uses genuine ML** for predictive deal intelligence
- **Bulk wholesale list analysis** remains underserved in most tools
- **Mobile experiences are universally weak** — even SellerAmp's "best-in-class" mobile app has chronic loading failures
- **VA/team workflow management** is an afterthought everywhere except Seller Assistant and BuyBotPro

## What sellers dream about when they imagine the perfect tool

Across Reddit, seller forums, and app reviews, the same wishes appear repeatedly. The dominant theme is **tool consolidation fatigue**. Serious OA/RA sellers currently run 4–6 separate subscriptions: Keepa for historical data ($21), SellerAmp or BuyBotPro for analysis ($20–55), Tactical Arbitrage for deal finding ($69), InventoryLab for prep/shipping ($69), a repricer ($15–99), and Sellerboard for profit tracking ($15–31). That's **$200+/month in tool costs** before selling a single unit.

Sellers consistently request **AI-powered "should I buy this?" scoring** with configurable risk tolerance — not just data presentation, but predictive recommendations. They want to know: Where will this product's price be in 60 days? How many sellers will pile on? Is this a seasonal product? Will this brand start enforcing IP claims? No tool currently answers these questions.

**End-to-end workflow management** is a universal wish — from product analysis through purchase decision, purchase order generation, prep instructions, and FBA shipment creation. Currently this requires SellerAmp + InventoryLab + manual work. Seller Assistant has begun building toward this with supplier databases and PO generation, validating the demand.

For the growing segment of multi-channel sellers, **cross-platform intelligence** is badly wanted: automatic price comparison across Amazon, eBay, Walmart, and retail sites, with cashback/coupon integration built into profit calculations. Google Shopping integration for finding the cheapest source. The ability to answer "should I sell this on Amazon or eBay?" instantly.

The most passionate requests come from scaling sellers who need **VA management built into the sourcing workflow**: sub-accounts with granular permissions, task assignment and tracking, approval workflows for purchase decisions, activity logging, and audit trails — all without sharing Seller Central credentials.

## What to bundle versus what to integrate or ignore

The evidence strongly supports a **"core bundle + specialist integrations"** strategy rather than trying to build an all-in-one suite. The key principle: **bundle tools used at the same workflow step** (the sourcing decision moment), **integrate with tools used at adjacent steps**, and **don't touch tools in different workflows entirely**.

**Build into the core product:** IP/brand risk checking (critical at the sourcing decision point — this is the #1 natural integration), restriction/gating checks, basic profit tracking per product, eBay profitability analysis (side-by-side with Amazon), cross-platform price comparison, and supplier management for wholesale sellers. These features are all consumed at the exact moment a seller evaluates a product — separating them creates unnecessary friction.

**Integrate via API/export but don't build from scratch:** Repricing (connect to RepricerExpress, BQool, Seller Snap — it's a mature, specialized market), inventory management (export buy lists to InventoryLab or CSV), cross-listing to eBay (partner with Vendoo or Crosslist rather than building a lister), and full bookkeeping (QuickBooks/Xero export). These are adjacent workflow steps where dedicated tools already excel.

**Explicitly avoid:** Listing optimization and PPC management (Helium 10 and Jungle Scout own this for private label), full cross-listing platforms (Vendoo and List Perfectly are mature), FBA prep/shipping workflows (InventoryLab dominates), and review/feedback automation (FeedbackWhiz). These serve different seller personas or different workflow stages, and building them creates bloat without competitive advantage.

The Seller 365 bundle at $69/month (10 tools including Tactical Arbitrage + InventoryLab) validates that the market wants consolidation. But that bundle targets the full OA operational workflow. Your tool should go **deeper on the sourcing decision** while going **wider across marketplaces** — a positioning no current bundle addresses.

## The eBay opportunity is wide open and growing

**No major Amazon sourcing tool supports eBay.** This is remarkable given that 40% of Amazon sellers also sell on eBay and Profit Scraper (the leading Amazon-to-eBay arbitrage tool) **shut down in December 2024** due to eBay API policy changes, creating a fresh market gap.

The highest-value eBay feature is a **dual-platform profitability calculator** that shows, for any product, the net profit on Amazon FBA, Amazon FBM, and eBay side-by-side. This means calculating eBay's Final Value Fees (which vary by category from 3–15%), payment processing fees (2.35% + $0.30), and Promoted Listings costs alongside Amazon's referral and FBA fees. Algopix attempts this across 17 marketplaces but has a **67/100 user satisfaction score** — poor execution of a great concept.

A **"Where to sell" recommendation engine** is the killer feature: based on price differentials, competition levels, sell-through rates, fee structures, and category restrictions, the tool should output "Best marketplace for this product is [eBay/Amazon FBA/Amazon FBM] because..." This fills a gap no tool adequately addresses.

**Cross-platform arbitrage detection** — automatically identifying products significantly cheaper on one platform than another — is highly valued but poorly served since Profit Scraper's closure. Building alerts for both eBay-to-Amazon and Amazon-to-eBay opportunities would be genuinely differentiated.

The main technical challenge is accessing eBay sold/completed listings data. eBay's Marketplace Insights API (Terapeak equivalent) is **restricted to approved developer partners** — regular developers cannot access sold item data at scale. Workarounds include building a proprietary historical database from active listings, partnering with eBay for Marketplace Insights access, or using third-party aggregators. This gating actually creates a moat: once you secure access, competitors can't easily replicate the feature.

eBay-specific features sellers want include category-specific fee intelligence (eBay categories have wildly different fee structures), product identifier cross-referencing (ASIN → UPC/EAN → eBay item specifics), and eBay competition analysis showing how many sellers list a given product, their price ranges, and feedback scores.

## Technical architecture and the $1,400 SP-API curveball

The technical foundation requires five major components: Amazon's SP-API, the Keepa API, eBay APIs, a Chrome extension (Manifest V3), and a mobile app with barcode scanning.

**Amazon SP-API** is the backbone, providing real-time pricing, fee estimates, Buy Box data, catalog information, and restriction checking. A critical development announced November 2025: **Amazon will charge third-party developers $1,400/year** starting January 31, 2026, plus tiered usage fees starting April 30, 2026. The Basic tier provides 2.5M GET calls/month for free (above the annual fee), while the Pro tier costs $1,000/month for 25M calls. This significantly increases costs for SaaS tools but doesn't affect personal use — a strategic advantage for building a personal tool first. Key rate limits: `getCompetitivePricing` at 10/sec with burst of 20, `getItemOffers` at 5/sec with burst of 10, and fee estimates at 1/sec.

**Keepa API** fills the critical gap SP-API doesn't cover: historical price data, BSR trends, Buy Box seller history, and sales estimates derived from BSR changes. Keepa bills on a token system where 1 token ≈ 1 product lookup, with batch requests up to 100 ASINs. API pricing ranges from **€49/month** (~$54, 20 tokens/minute) to **€4,499/month** (~$4,950, 4,000 tokens/minute). For a SaaS analyzing ~30K products/day, expect **€1,000–1,500/month** in Keepa costs — making it the largest variable cost at scale.

**eBay APIs** present the biggest technical challenge. The Browse API provides active listing data with application-level auth, but **sold/completed listings data is gated** behind the Marketplace Insights API, which requires eBay developer partnership approval. The legacy Finding API's `findCompletedItems` endpoint is effectively deprecated. Plan to apply for Marketplace Insights access early in development.

For the **Chrome extension**, Manifest V3 is mandatory. Key constraints: service workers (replacing persistent background pages) terminate after ~30 seconds of inactivity, no remote code execution allowed, and all JS must be bundled. The architecture pattern is: content script extracts ASIN/UPC from the page → sends to service worker → service worker calls backend API → backend queries SP-API + Keepa + cache → returns aggregated analysis → content script renders overlay. Use `chrome.storage.local` (10MB limit) for client-side caching with tiered TTLs: 24 hours for static data (titles, images), 1–4 hours for BSR/reviews, and 15–30 minutes for prices.

For **mobile**, React Native is the recommended framework for code sharing with the web/Chrome extension TypeScript ecosystem. For barcode scanning, start with `react-native-vision-camera` + Google ML Kit (free, good accuracy) and upgrade to **Scandit** for enterprise reliability if needed — Scandit achieves **26–167% longer scanning range** than ML Kit. Critical mobile UX requirements: open-to-scan in under 1 second, haptic/audio feedback, batch scanning mode, offline database for poor-connectivity stores, and Bluetooth scanner support for high-volume RA.

**Infrastructure costs scale predictably:**

| Stage | Users | Monthly Cost |

| Personal tool | 1 | **$105–170** |
| Early SaaS | 100–1,000 | **$1,900–3,400** |
| Growth stage | 1,000–10,000 | **$13,000–20,000** |

The recommended tech stack: **TypeScript** across Chrome extension (React + MV3), mobile (React Native + Expo), and web (Next.js), with a Node.js/NestJS backend, Python/FastAPI for ML services, PostgreSQL for primary data, Redis for caching, and AWS infrastructure (ECS Fargate, RDS, ElastiCache, SQS).

## The seven differentiators that would make sellers switch

Based on the full competitive analysis, community feedback, and market gaps, here are the features ranked by switching impact:

**1. AI-powered predictive deal scoring.** No tool currently uses genuine ML to predict future price trajectories, demand shifts, competitive entry likelihood, or IP risk probability. BuyBotPro's "AI" is rules-based, not learned. Build a weighted multi-factor scoring system starting rule-based (ROI, BSR trend, competition, Buy Box stability, price volatility, IP risk, category performance), then enhance with gradient boosting (XGBoost) trained on actual deal outcomes. The output: *"This deal has an 87% probability of maintaining profitability for 60+ days"* — fundamentally different from SellerAmp showing raw BSR numbers.

**2. Unified Amazon + eBay sourcing analysis.** Scan a product once, see profitability on Amazon FBA, Amazon FBM, and eBay side-by-side with marketplace-specific fee calculations, demand estimates, and a "where to sell" recommendation. No competitor does this. The December 2024 shutdown of Profit Scraper leaves the cross-platform arbitrage space especially underserved.

**3. Sub-2-second analysis speed.** Implement a hybrid data architecture: pre-cached database (updated hourly) delivers instant base results, with real-time API enrichment for current price/stock/restriction data. Scoutly proves offline databases can deliver sub-1-second results; combining this with live enrichment beats every current tool's latency.

**4. A mobile app that actually works.** Native React Native app (not a wrapped web view) with camera-first design — open the app, you're scanning. Swipe-right to add to buy list, swipe-left to reject. Offline database for stores with poor connectivity. Bluetooth scanner support. Voice notes for hands-free annotation. SellerAmp's mobile being the "best available" while having a 3.7/5 rating shows how low the bar is.

**5. Transparent data accuracy with confidence scores.** Display accuracy confidence for each metric: *"Sales estimate: ~150/mo ± 30, confidence: 78%."* Show data freshness timestamps on every data point. Cross-reference BSR trends, price history, review velocity, and competitor stock as multiple validation signals. Let users flag inaccurate data points to improve quality over time.

**6. Built-in VA/team workflow management.** Deal pipeline as a Kanban board (Found → Analyzing → Approved → Ordered → Shipped → Listed), VA task assignment with productivity tracking, approval workflows, auto-populated buy sheets, and team accounts with granular permissions. Sellers currently cobble this together across Trello, Google Sheets, and Slack.

**7. Proactive compliance intelligence.** Amazon is tightening enforcement dramatically: mandatory facial recognition for verification, expanded GS1 barcode requirements, increasing brand gating, ending FBA prep services in January 2026, and basing reimbursements on sourcing cost rather than selling price (March 2025). A tool that predicts which brands are likely to gate soon (based on IP claim velocity patterns), tracks compliance requirements per ASIN, and alerts when policies change affecting existing inventory has high perceived value in this environment.
